How to upscale an old, low-resolution video to 4K for free

Old phone clips, DVD rips and early camcorder videos look soft and choppy on today's screens. Here is the short answer, the exact steps, and what to expect from AI upscaling on an ordinary Windows PC.

Step by step

How to upscale an old video to 4K

  1. Install AI Video Upscaler+Get it free from the Microsoft Store and open it from the Start menu.
  2. Add your videoClick + Add Video and pick the file: an old phone clip, a DVD rip, a digitized tape, anything. Lower-quality sources are where the difference is biggest.
  3. Choose 4K and the frame rateSet Output resolution to 2160p (4K). For choppy footage, set Frame rate to Interpolate to 60 fps.
  4. Optional: faces, trim and rotateTurn up Face restoration if people are the focus, and use Trim / Rotate to keep only the part you need. That also saves processing time.
  5. Add to Queue and keep workingProcessing runs in the background on your PC. A Windows notification tells you when it's done, and the finished video is in Projects and in your Videos\AI Video Upscaler+ folder.

What to expect

Which old videos improve the most?

  • Old phone videos (640×480, 24–30 fps): the biggest visible change, since both resolution and motion improve.
  • DVD rips (480p / 576p): cleaner edges and text, ready for a 4K TV.
  • Digitized camcorder tapes and low-quality web clips (360p–480p).

AI reconstructs detail that is partly there. Videos with very heavy compression blocks, strong motion blur or very dark scenes improve less, so try a short trimmed part first.

Real numbers

How long does it take?

Tested on an ordinary laptop with an Intel Core i7-11800H, Intel UHD integrated graphics and 16 GB RAM, with no dedicated graphics card.
